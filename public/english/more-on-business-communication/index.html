More on Business Communication

January 11, 2025

3.1.4. Objectives of Business Communication

The objectives of business communication are as follows:

  1. To Give and Receive Information: Communication is used by organizations to convey important information to employees about goals, policies, and rules through meetings, telephone, notices, employee handbooks, and bulletins. Similarly, organizations use communication to interact with external parties such as investors, customers, suppliers, and the government, informing them about organizational strategies and efforts, either verbally or in written form.

  2. To Convey the Right Message: A key objective is to transmit the correct message to the recipient. The message should be clear, and it must be ensured that the person receiving the message fully understands it.

  3. To Provide Advice: Advice can be given on both personal and office matters. In business, advice is typically offered by supervisors to workers on how to operate machinery or equipment. Managers may seek expert advice on topics such as taxation, project finance, quality control, and engineering.

  4. To Receive Suggestions: In an organization, suggestions are encouraged to avoid ambiguity in communication. It is often a voluntary and sometimes anonymous process. Suggestions act as feedback and signify a smooth communication flow. Key sources of suggestions include customers and employees, as they are in direct contact with business operations.

  5. To Persuade People: Persuasion is the art of influencing attitudes, opinions, and actions. Effective persuasion is a critical goal of communication and requires strong speaking and writing skills. Face-to-face communication often enhances the effectiveness of persuasion.

  6. To Motivate People: Motivation drives individuals to work hard toward achieving organizational goals. A supportive, cooperative, and trust-based environment is necessary for motivation, which can be fostered through communication.

  7. To Provide Counselling: Counselling is a focused and systematic activity that requires professional knowledge and objectivity. Employees facing personal or family issues may experience decreased efficiency and confidence, and thus require counselling.

  8. To Issue Orders and Instructions: For accomplishing tasks, managers issue orders and instructions to employees. An order is a command to do something, while an instruction outlines how to carry out the task. Orders specify the task, while instructions explain how to complete it.

3.1.5. Process of Communication

Communication is a two-way process involving the exchange of ideas with mutual understanding. The communication process occurs between two participants via a medium that conveys the message, ensuring it is understandable to both sender and receiver. The communication process is depicted below:

  1. Source/Sender: The individual, group, or organization that initiates the communication is known as the sender or encoder. The sender is responsible for framing the message, choosing the medium, and determining the receiver. The effectiveness of the message depends on the sender’s traits, such as experience, behavior, knowledge, and cultural background.

  2. Message: The message is the encoded idea or information that the sender communicates. Messages can be verbal (written or spoken) or non-verbal (body language, appearance, silence, signs, etc.).

  3. Encoding: Encoding is the process of converting the sender’s thoughts into symbols (words, gestures, etc.) that can be transmitted. It’s important to consider how the message will be interpreted by the receiver and how it will impact the relationship between sender and receiver.

  4. Channel: The channel is the medium through which the message travels from sender to receiver. Channels can be mass media (e.g., newspapers, radio) or individual communication tools (e.g., telephone, emails). The choice of channel depends on the message’s importance, the number of recipients, and the available resources.

  5. Receiver: The receiver is the target of the message. They interpret the message by decoding it, understanding, and perceiving its meaning.

  6. Decoding: Decoding is the process where the receiver interprets the encoded message. The receiver applies their knowledge, experience, or consults external resources (e.g., dictionaries) to understand the message. Misinterpretation can lead to misunderstandings and disrupt the communication process.

  7. Feedback: Feedback is the response from the receiver to the sender. This indicates whether the message was understood clearly or if there were any misinterpretations.

  8. Noise: Noise refers to any interference that disrupts the communication process. It can occur at any stage and can hinder the effectiveness of the message.

3.1.6. Importance of Business Communication

Communication is a key component of the management process. The success of an organization heavily relies on effective communication. The importance of business communication includes:

  1. Basis of Decision-Making and Planning: Communication is vital for making informed decisions and planning effectively. Management decisions rely on accurate communication, both within the organization and with external stakeholders.

  2. Ensures Continuous and Efficient Working: Effective communication ensures that work is carried out efficiently and without interruption. It allows managers to guide and regulate employee activities.

  3. Ensures Proper Coordination: Communication fosters coordination by exchanging ideas and information, helping individuals work together toward a common goal.

  4. Improves Managerial Efficiency: A manager’s efficiency depends on their ability to communicate effectively. Clear communication helps allocate tasks, provide instructions, and assess performance.

  5. Promotes Cordial Industrial Relations: Communication fosters mutual understanding and cooperation between employees and employers, promoting a positive work environment.

  6. Helps in Establishing Effective Leadership: Effective communication is essential for leadership. It strengthens the relationship between managers and subordinates, facilitating effective leadership.

  7. Motivates and Boosts Morale: Communication can uplift employee morale by addressing their concerns, providing feedback, and fostering a sense of belonging.

  8. Ensures Effective Control: Communication serves as a tool for control. It allows managers to communicate plans, monitor performance, and take corrective actions.

  9. Provides Job Satisfaction: Effective communication helps build trust between employees and employers, fostering job satisfaction.

  10. Facilitates Democratic Management: Communication enables worker participation in decision-making, promoting a democratic approach in organizational management.

3.1.7. Essentials of Effective Business Communication

Effective communication is shaped by the following factors:

  1. Clarity of Purpose: Every communication should have a clear purpose, which must be understood by both the sender and receiver. There should be no confusion about the goal of the communication.

  2. Known Target Audience: The sender must understand the receiver’s characteristics, needs, and expectations to tailor the message effectively.

  3. Adequate Information: The message must provide sufficient and accurate information. The sender should consider the content and structure of the message to ensure it meets the receiver’s expectations.

  4. Effective Planning: Communication needs careful planning to be effective. This includes choosing the right content, words, and feedback mechanisms.

  5. Avoid Extreme Feelings: Negative emotions like anger or frustration should not affect the message. Such feelings can distort the message and harm relationships.

  6. Sincerity: Communication should be sincere and honest. Manipulating or misleading the receiver damages trust and credibility.

  7. Time Factor: Timing plays a crucial role in communication. Sending and receiving messages at the right time can significantly enhance the effectiveness of the message.

  8. Appropriate Communication Channels and Modes: The choice of communication method should match the situation and audience. The right medium can make the message more effective.

  9. Cost Consciousness: Communication should be mindful of costs, both direct and indirect. Effective communication strategies take into account the available budget.
