Business Communication

BUSINESS COMMUNICATION

3.1.1 Meaning and Definition of Communication

The term communication originates from the Latin word ‘communicare’, meaning sharing, contributing, informing, popularizing, and spreading. Communication is a process where individuals (two or more) share views, suggestions, evidence, thoughts, and feelings. The parties involved in this process are known as the sender and receiver.

Communication involves sharing information and achieving mutual understanding among individuals at the same or different levels. In an organizational context, management uses communication to assign and control work, motivate employees, and align individual and group performance with organizational goals.

Non Verbal Communication

Nonverbal Communication

According to Vahugan and Hogg, nonverbal communication is the transfer of meaningful information from one person to another through means other than written or spoken words.

Characteristics of Nonverbal Communication

  • Primarily conveys emotional attitudes.
  • Acts as a substitute for or an emphasis on verbal communication.
  • Continuous process rather than discrete events.
  • More reliable as nonverbal cues are often authentic and involuntary.

Main Components of Nonverbal Communication

  1. Paralanguage (Vocalics)
  2. Kinesics (Body Language)
  3. Haptics (Touch Communication)
  4. Proxemics (Space Language)
  5. Chronemics (Time Management)
  6. Symbol/Sign Language

Paralanguage

Paralanguage refers to how a message is delivered rather than the content of the message itself.

Barriers in Communication

BARRIERS TO COMMUNICATION

Barriers to effective communication can arise from various sources. These include:

  1. Semantic Barriers
  2. Physical Barriers
  3. Cultural Barriers
  4. Emotional and Psychological Barriers
  5. Socio-Psychological Barriers
  6. Technological Barriers
  7. Organizational Barriers
  8. Personal Barriers

SEMANTIC BARRIERS

Semantic barriers occur due to differences in understanding the meaning of words, symbols, or actions. Examples include:

  • Use of different words or terms
  • Language differences
  • Misinterpretation of pictures or visuals
  • Poor vocabulary
  • Confusing or unclear actions

Communication Introduction

COMMUNICATION

Communication is the process of exchanging information, ideas, thoughts, emotions, and feelings through speech, signals, writing, or behavior.

TYPES OF COMMUNICATION

  • Verbal Communication
  • Non-Verbal Communication

LEVELS OF COMMUNICATION

  • Intrapersonal Communication:
    Communication that occurs within one’s own mind, influenced by personal feelings, biases, prejudices, and beliefs.

  • Interpersonal Communication:
    Interaction between two people, which can extend to informal conversations involving more participants.

  • One-to-Group Communication:
    Involves a speaker addressing an audience with the aim of informing or motivating them.
