BUSINESS COMMUNICATION

3.1.1 Meaning and Definition of Communication

The term communication originates from the Latin word ‘communicare’, meaning sharing, contributing, informing, popularizing, and spreading. Communication is a process where individuals (two or more) share views, suggestions, evidence, thoughts, and feelings. The parties involved in this process are known as the sender and receiver.

Communication involves sharing information and achieving mutual understanding among individuals at the same or different levels. In an organizational context, management uses communication to assign and control work, motivate employees, and align individual and group performance with organizational goals.

Communication also fosters effective interaction among individuals to address, implement, or resolve specific situations. It leads to harmony by aligning common goals, interests, and actions within an organization.

  • Newman and Summer: “Communication is an exchange of facts, ideas, opinions, or emotions by two or more persons.”
  • Keith Davis: “Communication is a process of passing information and understanding from one person to another.”
  • American Management Association: “Communication is any behavior that results in an exchange of meaning.”
  • Peter Little: “Communication is the process by which information is transmitted between individuals and/or organizations so that an understanding response results.”

3.1.2 Nature of Business Communication

The nature of business communication is outlined below:

  1. Specialized Form of Communication
    Business communication incorporates industrial and commercial issues, making it a specialized form of standard communication.

  2. Presence of Formal Aspects
    Factors such as the use of graphics, audiovisual aids, commercial vocabulary, and standardized formats are emphasized.

  3. Practical Information
    Focuses on providing actionable insights, such as reasons for procedural changes, current project progress, or root causes of issues.

  4. Clear and Concise Information
    Charts, graphs, tables, and images are used to convey precise and focused information.

  5. Formality
    Business communication is generally formal in nature, unlike casual communication.

  6. Problem-Solving
    Communication aids in finding solutions to organizational problems, such as negotiations or stakeholder meetings.

  7. Facilitates Change
    Transparency in communication ensures smooth implementation of organizational changes.

  8. Gate Keeping
    Effective communication connects an organization to the external environment, enhancing its effectiveness.

3.1.3 Scope of Communication

The scope of communication spans various aspects of life and work:

  1. In Personal Life
    Communication is essential for everyday activities and relationships.

  2. In Social Life
    Strengthens societal connections and preserves social relations.

  3. In Industry
    Coordinates resources like manpower, machinery, materials, and methods.

  4. In Business
    A fundamental requirement for all business functions and public engagement.

  5. In Management
    Acts as the backbone for planning, organizing, implementing, and decision-making processes.

  6. In Politics
    Utilized in government policies, political campaigns, and public messages.

  7. In Religion
    Promotes the values and teachings of religious beliefs.

  8. In Organizational Behavior
    Builds and maintains healthy relationships between employees and employers.

  9. In International Relations
    Enables global interactions through cooperative institutions like SAARC, EU, NAFTA, and ASEAN.

  10. In Industrial Relations
    Strengthens transparency between labor unions and management for organizational success.

  11. In Decision-Making
    Provides critical information needed for informed decisions.

  12. In Publicity
    Promotes products and services through effective mass communication.

  13. In Information Exchange
    Facilitates meaningful exchanges of information critical for individuals and organizations.
